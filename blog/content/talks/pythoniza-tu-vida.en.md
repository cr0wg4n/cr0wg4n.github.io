## Summary

Python is one of the easiest languages to learn: its libraries and syntax let you do amazing things with little effort. Still, we almost always use it only to solve problems at work, or as one more language at university. There are many other things you can do for yourself with Python, and in this talk I share a few ideas to "pythonize", that is, automate, part of our everyday activities.

Knowing a programming language is like knowing how to talk to the computer. If we stretch the idea a little, the computer can work as a second brain. Why not let it solve problems for us in the background?

## What it covers

I start with everyday problems: the downloads folder that is always a mess, the aches from spending hours in a bad posture in front of the computer, or repetitive tasks such as taking screenshots of several dashboards one by one.

From there I suggest automating whatever we can, so technology works in the background and we use that time for something more productive, or simply to rest. To show it, I share two small projects:

- **A directory organizer**, which sorts the downloads automatically.
- **An exercise reminder**, which every 60 minutes suggests an active break or a calisthenics routine.

## Fun Driven Development

I also present a model of my own, Fun Driven Development, built around motivation and enjoying the process. Unlike a work methodology, it does not answer to productivity metrics: what matters is having a good time while you learn and solve. It has four stages:

1. **Analysis and research:** understand the variables of the problem and research how to solve it, always asking whether the process is fun.
2. **Planning:** choose the technologies and tools without losing sight of personal satisfaction.
3. **Development and testing:** implement the solution and test it, enjoying this part too despite the technical challenges.
4. **Solution:** reach the expected result and move on to a new challenge, or improve what you learned.

And if a problem cannot or should not be automated, there is still a way out: work on social skills and change your perspective to face it differently.
